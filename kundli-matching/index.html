the eight kootas

what each of the 36 points is actually for.

Every koota is computed from the Moon's nakshatra and sign in each chart — nothing else enters the calculation. The points are not evenly weighted, and the two biggest carry more than half the total between them.

kootapointswhat it compares
varna1a fourfold grouping of the Moon signs. Scores only when the groom's group is not below the bride's in the classical order.
vashya2a five-way grouping of the signs — human, quadruped, aquatic, wild, insect — read as mutual influence.
tara3counts the nakshatra distance between the two charts in both directions; some remainders are auspicious, some are not.
yoni4each nakshatra maps to one of fourteen animal symbols with a sex; the score is a table of how each pair relates.
graha maitri5friendship between the lords of the two Moon signs, read both ways and combined.
gana6a three-way temperament grouping of the nakshatras — deva, manushya, rakshasa.
bhakoot7the distance between the two Moon signs. Certain axes — 6/8 and 5/9 — score zero and are called bhakoot dosha.
nadi8a three-way grouping — aadi, madhya, antya. Sharing one scores zero, the single largest loss available, subject to classical exemptions.

two conventions here are genuinely disputed between classical sources: the vashya matrix and the mixed cells of graha maitri, worth 2 and 5 points respectively. shastra uses the mainstream Indian implementation and records the disagreement in its code rather than hiding it — which is why your score may differ by a point or two from a site that chose the other convention.

about the thresholds

18 out of 36 is a convention, not a result.

what the tradition says

In common Indian practice, 18 or more out of 36 is regarded as acceptable, 25 or more as strong, and below 18 as weak. Nadi and bhakoot — 8 and 7 points — are weighted heavily enough that a total above 18 which lost both is usually read differently from one that lost neither.

what this page will not do

Turn that into a verdict about your relationship. Guna milan compares two Moon positions. It has no information about two people who already know each other, and classical astrology itself treats it as one input among several. shastra reports the number, shows the arithmetic, and stops there — no pass or fail, and nothing sold to "fix" a low score.

matching questions, answered

frequently asked

What is guna milan and how is it scored?

Guna milan, also called ashtakoota milan, is the vedic method of comparing two birth charts for marriage. It scores eight factors — kootas — out of 36 points total: varna 1, vashya 2, tara 3, yoni 4, graha maitri 5, gana 6, bhakoot 7 and nadi 8.

Every koota is derived from the Moon's nakshatra and sign in each chart, and nothing else. The whole calculation is arithmetic on lookup tables, which is why any two correct implementations must produce the same number.

How many gunas out of 36 are considered a good match?

The conventional reading is 18 or more out of 36 as acceptable, 25 or more as strong, and below 18 as weak — with the caveat that nadi (8 points) and bhakoot (7 points) carry outsized weight, so a total above 18 that lost both is read differently from one that lost neither.

Those thresholds are convention, not arithmetic. They summarise what the tradition has settled on; they are not something the calculation produces. shastra reports the number and the breakdown and does not issue a verdict on a relationship.

What is nadi dosha?

Nadi dosha occurs when both Moon nakshatras fall in the same nadi group — aadi, madhya or antya. Nadi is worth 8 of the 36 points, the largest single koota, and a match sharing a nadi scores zero for it.

Classical sources list exemptions: the two charts sharing the same Moon sign, the same nakshatra with different padas, or the nakshatra lords being the same. shastra checks those and reports which, if any, applied — rather than reporting the dosha and leaving you to find out about the exemptions elsewhere.

What is mangal dosha, and am I manglik?

Mangal dosha means Mars occupies the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th house from a reference point in the chart. Practice differs on which reference to use, so shastra checks all three that classical sources actually cite — the lagna, the Moon and Venus — and reports each separately instead of picking one and calling it settled.

It also applies the classical cancellations, such as Mars occupying its own sign or an exalted one. Mangal dosha is not part of the 36-point score; it is a separate check reported alongside it.

Does kundli matching need exact birth times?

Guna milan needs only the Moon's position, which depends on the date far more than the clock — so a missing birth time rarely changes the score, and never by much.

Mangal dosha is different: part of that check is made from the lagna, which moves a full sign roughly every two hours. With an unknown birth time the lagna-based half of it is approximate, and this page says so rather than presenting both at the same confidence.

Is kundli matching required for marriage?

No. It is a widespread custom in Indian marriage arrangement, not a legal requirement, and classical astrology itself weighs it as one input among several rather than as a decision.

Guna milan compares Moon positions and nothing else. It knows nothing about two people who already know each other.

Why does my score differ from another website's?

Almost always one of two conventions. The vashya matrix (2 points) and the mixed cells of graha maitri (5 points) are genuinely disputed between classical sources, and different implementations pick differently. A one or two point difference from those is expected.

A larger gap usually means a different ayanamsa, which can move a Moon into a neighbouring nakshatra and change several kootas at once. shastra uses Lahiri, the Indian standard.
