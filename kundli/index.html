kundli questions, answered

frequently asked

What is a kundli?

A kundli, also called a janam kundli or birth chart, is a diagram of where the planets stood in the sky at the moment and place you were born. In vedic astrology it records the twelve houses, the twelve zodiac signs, the nine grahas (Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rahu and Ketu), and the nakshatra, one of 27 lunar mansions, occupied by each. It is computed arithmetically from astronomical data, so two correct kundlis for the same birth details will always be identical.

What is Lahiri ayanamsa?

Lahiri ayanamsa, also called Chitrapaksha ayanamsa, is the offset between the tropical zodiac (measured from the March equinox) and the sidereal zodiac (measured against the fixed stars) used in Indian vedic astrology. It is defined by placing the star Spica (Chitra) at exactly 180° of sidereal longitude. The offset is currently about 24°, and it grows by roughly 50.29 arcseconds a year because the Earth's axis precesses. It was adopted as the Indian national standard by the Calendar Reform Committee in 1955.

This is why a vedic chart usually places your Sun one sign earlier than a western horoscope does.

What if I don't know my exact birth time?

Tick the unknown-time box and the chart is computed from sunrise on your birth date. What stays reliable is everything driven by the date rather than the clock: your Moon sign and nakshatra, and the sign of every planet except in the rare case where the Moon changes sign that day.

What becomes approximate is your lagna, which moves a full sign about every two hours, the house positions that follow from it, and your vimshottari dasha dates, which can shift by months. shastra prints that caveat on the chart rather than silently defaulting to noon and presenting the result as exact.

Why is my vedic sun sign different from my western one?

Because the two systems measure from different starting points. Western astrology uses the tropical zodiac, anchored to the March equinox. Vedic astrology uses the sidereal zodiac, anchored to the fixed stars. The Earth's axis wobbles, so those two reference points have drifted about 24° apart over roughly two millennia, a little under one full sign.

That is why someone born in late March is Aries in a western horoscope and usually Pisces in a vedic one. Neither is a mistake. They answer to different definitions of where the zodiac begins.

What is a nakshatra?

A nakshatra is one of 27 equal divisions of the zodiac, each 13°20' wide, roughly the distance the Moon travels in a day, which is why they are called lunar mansions. Each is divided into four padas of 3°20'.

Your Moon's nakshatra is the single most used value in Indian astrology: it sets your vimshottari dasha sequence, it is what kundli matching actually compares, and it traditionally supplies the first syllable of a child's name.

What is vimshottari dasha?

Vimshottari dasha divides life into planetary periods totalling 120 years. Each graha rules a mahadasha of fixed length: Ketu 7 years, Venus 20, Sun 6, Moon 10, Mars 7, Rahu 18, Jupiter 16, Saturn 19, Mercury 17.

Which one you start on, and how much of it had already elapsed when you were born, is set by the exact position of your Moon inside its nakshatra. Each mahadasha then subdivides into antardashas in the same proportions and the same order.
